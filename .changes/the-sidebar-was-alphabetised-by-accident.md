# fixed

A third of the documentation sidebar was ordered by file name rather than by
anybody's decision.

Starlight sorts an autogenerated group by each page's `sidebar.order` and
breaks a tie on the file name. 27 of the 78 ordered pages shared a number with
a sibling, so the reader saw an alphabetical order that nobody chose, and the
author could not see it, because they were looking at a title and the tie was
decided by a slug.

The damage was not abstract. "Watching a run" sat between "The local runtime"
and "The Kubernetes runtime" and split the pair. "Provider limits" sat between
DBLab and Supabase and split the three provider pages. The two Next.js guides
were separated by Django, Synthesis and a page about model keys. "On-call" came
before "Standing up production", which is the wrong way round for somebody
reading the self-hosting section in order. Reference ran 1, 1, 6, 7, 8, 9, 9
with four numbers unused, and self-hosting carried a 3.5 wedged between two
pages that both claimed 3.

Every group is now contiguous from 1 with no repeats, so a page added later has
an obvious place. Where the accident was harmless the order it already rendered
in was kept and simply made explicit; where it was not, the moves are: the two
runtime guides together, the framework guides together, the three provider
pages together with the cross-provider one last, production and operations
before on-call, Subsetting up beside Goldens and Masking rather than in a
three-way tie at the bottom, and the control plane's two reference pages
adjacent. Nothing else on the site moved, which was checked by diffing the
rendered sidebar of a real build before and against after.

`just sidebarcheck` now refuses a repeat, a fraction, a page with no order
among siblings that have one, and a nested group numbered below its parent.
That last one is not obvious and it happened during this change: Starlight
places a nested group at the smallest order of its children, so renumbering the
runbooks from 1 lifted the whole group above Azure.
